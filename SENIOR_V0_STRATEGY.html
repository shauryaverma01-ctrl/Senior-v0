Section 01 — the reframe

You are not building a voice agent. You are building the guest data layer for Indian hospitality, with the phone call as the wedge.

Every other framing — AI hostess, AI maître d', front-of-house OS — is marketing skin over that one structural truth. If a partner walks out of May 21 remembering one thing, it should be: Senior owns the inbound call → Senior owns the guest profile → Senior becomes the CRM, the loyalty engine, and eventually the revenue layer. The voice agent is the trojan horse. The guest graph is the product.

"Another voice AI for restaurants" is a category with eight funded teams in India and forty globally. "The guest operating system for Indian F&B, distributed via voice" is a category of one — and the moat compounds the moment you have a single restaurant's six-month call history sitting in your Postgres.

It anchors on a role (front-of-house), not a technology (voice AI). The upgrade path from "we answer your calls" to "we are your CRM, your loyalty stack, and your demand engine" is already baked in. Every later product is a natural extension, not a pivot.

Section 02 — the investor story in three sentences

Three sentences, in this order.

The wedge

Indian restaurants miss 30–40% of their calls; the ones they answer are taken by frontline staff with 60% annual churn, no memory of the caller, and zero ability to upsell. We replace that layer.

The structural insight

In the US, OpenTable owns the guest. In India, the phone owns the guest — there is no installed reservation graph. Whoever sits on the call captures the guest record by default, and the phone number is the universal key.

The compounding asset

Every call we handle adds a row to a guest table that didn't exist before. Within twelve months, a typical Senior restaurant has 3,000–8,000 structured guest profiles — preferences, allergies, occasions, visit cadence. Impossible to reconstruct if they churn.

The QA / mystery-shopping / DeepCall narrative does not lead. It is slide eleven, not slide three. Putting it earlier dilutes the core reframe — investors will pattern-match you to "voice eval startup" and you will lose the vertical-SaaS multiple.

Section 03 — the two pillars

Built on two structured memories. Both compound. Both proprietary per restaurant.

The Guest Graph is the structured memory of who walks in. The Restaurant Brain is the structured memory of what they walk into. One without the other is half a product.

Guest Graph — who comes here

Phone-keyed profile of every caller. Fields: name, phone_e164, first_seen_at, last_seen_at, visit_count, tier (new / regular / vip), preferences, allergens, occasions (birthday, anniversary), notes, last_visit_summary, risk_flags. One row per guest, growing every call. Compounds via every call.

Restaurant Brain — what they walk into

Structured menu ontology (dishes, ingredients, allergens, dietary tags, pairings, live availability) plus FAQ corpus (categorized, versioned) plus live kitchen state. The agent reasons over it during a call. Compounds via kitchen updates and FAQ-gap closure.

Section 04 — the restaurant brain in detail

Three layers, independently shippable, no new infrastructure.

Layer 1 — Menu Graph

Relational ontology in Supabase. Tables: dishes, dish_tags, dish_allergens, dish_pairings. SQL filters at sub-50ms over 40 dishes. No pgvector for V0 — overkill at this scale, deterministic and debuggable matters more.

Layer 2 — FAQ corpus

Categorized FAQ items with question_canonical, aliases, answer, expires_at. faq_gaps table captures every "let me check on that" — the self-improvement engine. Weekly Telegram digest with one-tap "Add to FAQ".

Layer 3 — Update channels

Kitchen Telegram chat: /86 lamb, /back lamb, /special truffle pasta. Manager Telegram for FAQ gaps. Owner-driven menu PDF ingestion for new restaurants — claims the onboarding wow moment ("30 minutes, not 30 days").

Why menu and FAQ aren't a single feature: 30–40% of weekday calls are not reservation calls — they're parking, hours, dress code, dietary, allergens. Without this pillar, Senior is worse than a tired host who at least knows the menu. With it, Senior reasons over the restaurant in real time.

01

Phone-keyed guests table

V0 ship effort 2 soph 9 0.5d

A new guests table in Supabase keyed on phone_e164 (unique). Foreign-keyed from calls.guest_id and reservations.guest_id. Holds: name, phone, first_seen_at, last_seen_at, visit_count, tier, preferences (jsonb), allergens (text[]), occasions (jsonb), notes, last_visit_summary, risk_flags.

Why it matters

The single primitive. Without it, every other feature is a hack on a flat call log. Today phone is free text on reservations — that's a column, not an entity. Promoting the phone number to its own row turns Senior from a logging tool into a CRM.

User value

Recognized by name. Doesn't repeat allergies. Same table as last time.

Restaurant value

First structured, queryable view of who guests actually are.

Investor value

"6 months in: 4,000 structured profiles that didn't exist before. We own the schema."

Reuse

_shared/phone.ts already gives E.164. Existing webhook upserts.

Fake MVP

None — just build it. This is the floor.

Data moat

The biggest one. Every call adds a row. Twelve months in, irreplaceable.

Unlocks

Loyalty. Birthday campaigns. WhatsApp marketing. Cross-restaurant guest passport.

RiskPhone-number ambiguity (shared household phone) — disambiguate by name. DPDP Act compliance — needs consent line + deletion endpoint. Cheap to add.

V0 verdict: Yes. Build it first. Tomorrow. Everything else is downstream of this or not worth doing.

Cross-flow architectural rules

  • Idempotency. Every webhook handler is idempotent on its primary key. calls uses ON CONFLICT (id) DO NOTHING per CLAUDE.md rule 5. guests UPSERT keyed on phone_e164. reservations dedup via (call_id, booking_date, booking_time).
  • Read-at-call-start. Live call reads guest context, menu state, FAQ corpus once at call start. Mid-call mutations (kitchen /86, manager edits) apply on the next call, not the current one. Avoids race conditions without distributed locks.
  • Always 200, never 500. Per CLAUDE.md rule 9 — every webhook returns 200 with descriptive body even on duplicate-retry. Zoronal's retry behavior never poisons the call log.
  • Manager-tap-Send wins. Any 1:1 outbound (recovery SMS, occasion SMS, wait-list fill) flows through a Telegram card with Send / Edit / Skip. Buys trust + tuning data + demo polish. Auto-send is a V1 conversion once tuned.
  • Async LLM never on critical path. Caller recall context fetch is sync (≤200ms). Every other LLM call (extractor, classifier, summarizer, QA scorer) is post-call async. Never blocks the caller.
  • Single source of truth. reservations.status is the source of truth for booking state. SMS delivery is best-effort; Telegram cards reflect status, not own it. Plivo timeouts don't poison the booking.
  • Latency budget on tool calls. menu_query <500ms, check_capacity <100ms (CLAUDE.md spec), faq_lookup <200ms. Indexes on (restaurant_id, is_available), (restaurant_id, category), (phone_e164), (booking_date, booking_time) are non-negotiable.
  • DPDP compliance. Opening line of every call includes "this call may be recorded for service quality." guests.consent_at tracks. Per-guest deletion endpoint exists. PII never leaves ap-south-1.
